summary: If God is all-good and all-powerful, why is there evil and suffering?
---

**The objection, at its strongest:**

1. If God were all-good, he would want to prevent evil.
2. If God were all-powerful, he would be able to prevent evil.
3. Yet evil plainly exists &mdash; not only human cruelty, but the anguish of disease, famine, and disaster.
4. Therefore a God who is both all-good and all-powerful does not exist.

This is the deepest objection, because it does not deal in abstractions.
It begins with a child's suffering, and no honest answer can pretend that away.

**In response:**

- **The logical form fails.** For the argument to work, it must assume that an all-good God *could have no reason* to permit any evil. But that is precisely what cannot be shown. If God can bring a greater good out of a permitted evil &mdash; goods we may not see from inside the story &mdash; then evil's existence and God's goodness are not contradictory.
- **Evil is not a "thing."** In the classical view evil is not a substance God created, but a *privation* &mdash; a lack of some good that ought to be present, as blindness is the absence of sight. God does not author evil; he permits the possibility of it as the shadow side of real goods like freedom and a lawful, causal world.
- **Free will has a price.** Genuine love and virtue are impossible without the freedom to refuse them. A world of puppets who could not do wrong would also be a world with no courage, forgiveness, or love freely given.
- **This is an answer, not an anesthetic.** Christianity does not offer a tidy theory that dissolves grief. It offers a God who enters the suffering himself, on a cross &mdash; which is a very different claim than "the suffering does not matter."
