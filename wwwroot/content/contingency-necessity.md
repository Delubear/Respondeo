Look at any ordinary thing — a tree, a star, yourself. Each **might not have
existed.** Its existence is not part of what it is; it is *received.*

**The argument, step by step:**

1. Contingent things exist — things that can come to be and pass away.
2. What is contingent does not account for its own existence; it depends on
   another for it.
3. If everything were contingent, the whole collection would still be
   unexplained — borrowed existence with no lender.
4. Therefore there must exist a Being whose existence is **necessary**, not
   received from another: *this all men speak of as God.*

This is why the question "what caused God?" misfires. God is not one more
contingent item needing a cause; He is the one whose very essence *is* to exist.
