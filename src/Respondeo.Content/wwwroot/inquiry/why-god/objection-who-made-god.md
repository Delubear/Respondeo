---
id: objection-who-made-god
title: "\"Who Made God?\""
summary: "If everything needs a cause, doesn't God need one too?"
---

**The objection, at its strongest:**

1. The argument for God says everything that exists needs a cause.
2. God exists.
3. So God needs a cause, and then that cause needs a cause, forever.
4. Either everything has a cause (so God is not special), or something can be uncaused (so why not the universe?).

**In response:**

- **It misstates the premise.** The classical arguments never say *everything* needs a cause. 
  They say everything *that begins to exist*, or everything *whose existence is dependent/contingent*, needs a cause. 
  God is not proposed as one more contingent item, but as the one necessary being whose essence *is* to exist. 
  Asking "who made the uncaused cause?" is like asking "what is north of the North Pole?"
- **This is not special pleading.** Special pleading would be exempting God for no reason. 
  But there *is* a reason: a chain of dependent causes cannot be dependent all the way down, or it never gets started. 
  The regress terminates in something non-dependent by definition: that is the whole point of the argument, not a cheat added at the end.

  St. Thomas makes exactly this move in the Second Way, and gives the uncaused cause its name:

  > There is no case known... in which a thing is found to be the efficient cause of itself;
  > for so it would be prior to itself, which is impossible... Now if it is possible to go on
  > to infinity in efficient causes, there will be no first efficient cause, neither will
  > there be an ultimate effect, nor any intermediate efficient causes; all of which is
  > plainly false. Therefore it is necessary to admit a first efficient cause, to which
  > everyone gives the name of God.
  >
  > &mdash; [*Summa Theologiae* I, Q. 2, A. 3](summa/prima-q002#article-3)

- **"Why not the universe?" is a fair question with an answer.** The universe is the very thing whose contingency we observe: 
  it changes, it is composite, it could have been otherwise. 
  Those are exactly the marks of something that *does* need explaining, not the marks of a necessary ground.

  It does not help to say the universe is *eternal*, or *self-contained*, or that physics finds some uncaused quantum vacuum at the bottom. 
  Those answers confuse **duration** with **necessity**. A universe that has always existed has merely been borrowing its existence for a longer time; 
  age does not turn a contingent thing into a necessary one, any more than a debt becomes your own money by being owed for a century. 
  And anything made of parts &mdash; fields, particles, space, time &mdash; depends on those parts being arranged as they are, and so still asks for a ground beyond itself. 
  Whatever physics names as the deepest layer, if it is composite and could have been otherwise, it is one more thing that needs explaining, not the explanation. 
  A necessary being is not the oldest or smallest item in the universe; it is of an entirely different kind: that whose essence simply *is* to exist.

*This is exactly the reasoning of [the Second Way &mdash; Causation](node/aquinas-five-ways?section=five-ways-causation).*
