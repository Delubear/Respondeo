Welcome. You're here because a question found you.

That alone is worth noticing.
To ask whether there is a God is to assume that the answer matters — that reality is not indifferent to being understood, and that getting it right is better than being comfortably wrong.
That restlessness has a name: it is the desire for truth, and it is the most honest thing in you.
A mind content with illusions would never have asked.

So the Catholic tradition meets you exactly where that desire begins — not by demanding faith, but by offering reason.

Following St. Thomas Aquinas, we hold that God's existence can be **demonstrated** from things you already observe: change, cause and effect, and the plain fact that anything exists at all.
These are not appeals to feeling. They are arguments you can examine, test, and push back on.

The claim we will weigh together is simple: the world around us does not explain itself, and what does fully explain it is what everyone means by *God*.

Holding off on a decision makes sense when the evidence is truly balanced.
But the question of God is not a coin toss — it asks whether reality has an ultimate explanation, and a desire for truth deserves to see where the evidence actually leads.

The Thomistic approach we'll take respects your caution:
- It does not ask you to *assume* God to get started.
- It asks only that you follow the evidence of change and existence wherever it leads.
- If the arguments fail, you should remain atheist or agnostic.
- If they succeed, atheism and agnosticism are no longer the honest position.

The only thing asked of you is a willingness to follow the evidence.
