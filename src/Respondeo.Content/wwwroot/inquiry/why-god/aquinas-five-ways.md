St. Thomas offered *Five Ways* to demonstrate God's existence.

They do not rest on faith or Scripture; each starts from a plain feature of the world and reasons its way to the source that must lie behind it.

Prefer a video introduction? The Thomistic Institute offers *Five Ways to Prove God Exists*.

:::

Two distinctions will help you follow Aquinas's reasoning.
The first is the difference between **act and potency** &mdash; what a thing actually is now, versus what it has the potential to become.
The second is the difference between **essence and existence** &mdash; what a thing is, versus the fact that it exists at all.

Don't worry if those names are unfamiliar. The first two sections explain them through ordinary examples, and the arguments return to them where they are needed.

Each Way begins with something familiar:

- **Motion:** Things change. What ultimately makes change possible?
- **Causation:** Causes depend on other causes. Can every cause receive its power from something else?
- **Contingency:** Things come into being and pass away. Does everything depend on something else for its existence?
- **Gradation:** Things possess goodness and other perfections in limited ways. What accounts for those perfections?
- **Governance:** Things without minds nevertheless act toward definite outcomes. Why does nature have this directedness?

These are five arguments, not five steps of one argument. You can explore each in its own right.
Their common thread is dependence: the things we encounter point toward a source that does not borrow what it gives.

Aquinas concludes each with God.
