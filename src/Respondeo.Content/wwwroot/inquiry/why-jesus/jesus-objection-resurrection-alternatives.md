**The objection, at its strongest:**

Even granting an empty tomb and sincere reports of appearances, a natural account is more probable than a miracle: perhaps the disciples hallucinated in grief, mistook the tomb, invented the story, or Jesus never truly died. Extraordinary claims require extraordinary evidence.

**The response:**

The demand for a natural explanation is fair; the test is whether any natural explanation fits *all* the evidence at once.
Taken one at a time, each alternative accounts for a fragment and breaks on the rest.

**Swoon** — that Jesus survived crucifixion.
Roman soldiers executed people for a living and confirmed His death; the spear thrust makes survival still less likely.
And a half-dead man crawling from a tomb would have inspired pity, not the conviction that He had conquered death.

**Hallucination** — that grief conjured the appearances.
Hallucinations are private, individual events; they are not shared by groups, on multiple occasions, in different settings.
They also do not produce an empty tomb, and they do not convert *skeptics* like Paul and James, who were not primed by grief to see anything at all.

**Theft or myth** — that the body was taken or the story invented.
This founders on the willingness of the witnesses to suffer and die for what they proclaimed.
Thieves do not die for a corpse they hid, and the claim arose too early, in the very city where the tomb could be checked, for myth to take hold.

**Wrong tomb** — that the mourners simply went to the wrong grave.
The tomb's location was known; the authorities, who had every motive to produce the body and end the movement, needed only to walk to the right one.
They never did.

**The cumulative point.** Each theory can be stretched to cover a single fact.
None covers the death, the empty tomb, the group appearances, the converted skeptics, *and* the willingness to die — all together.
"Extraordinary claims require extraordinary evidence" is a sound principle, and it cuts both ways: the *ordinary* explanations turn out to require extraordinary strain to hold the facts together.
The Resurrection is not believed for lack of alternatives, but because it explains what the alternatives cannot.
