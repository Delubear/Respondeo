St. Bernadette Soubirous, the visionary of Lourdes, entered the Sisters of Charity of Nevers in 1866
and died there in 1879 at the age of 35, after years of illness. Her body is among the best-known
cases of an incorrupt body venerated in the Catholic tradition.

## What happened

Bernadette's body was exhumed three times as part of the process toward her canonization: in 1909,
1919, and 1925. On each occasion the witnesses, who included physicians and civil officials as well as
clergy, reported the body to be remarkably preserved, with the muscles and internal organs still
supple decades after burial, even as the rosary in her hands had rusted and her habit had become damp.
After the final exhumation her body was placed in a glass reliquary at the Chapel of Saint-Gildard in
Nevers, where it can still be seen by pilgrims.

## What "incorrupt" means and does not mean

The Church attaches no doctrinal weight to bodily incorruption, and it is careful and honest about the
term. The faithful should know, for instance, that a light wax coating was made for the face and hands
before the body was put on display, because the skin had darkened; the wax is a mask over the real
remains, not a disguise of decay. It is also true that natural conditions can slow decomposition, so
the Church does not rush to call any single case supernatural. Incorruption is regarded as a notable
phenomenon associated with some saints, not as proof of sanctity. Sanctity is judged by a life of
heroic virtue, never by the state of a body.

## How the Church regards it

Bernadette was beatified in 1925 and canonized in 1933 on the basis of her holiness and the
examination of her cause, not because of the condition of her remains. The incorruption is venerated by
tradition as a sign that invites wonder; the canonization rests entirely on other grounds.

## Why it matters

The case illustrates the Church's measured approach: it venerates a striking sign without overstating
it, is transparent about the wax and about what nature can and cannot do, and grounds sainthood in a
person's life rather than in any marvel about their body.
