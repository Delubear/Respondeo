Our Lady of Guadalupe is one of the most widely venerated Marian apparitions, formally recognized by
the Church and profoundly significant to the evangelization of the Americas. Her shrine in Mexico City
is the most-visited Catholic shrine in the world.

## What happened

According to the tradition recorded in the Nican Mopohua, the Virgin Mary appeared to St. Juan Diego,
an indigenous Chichimec convert, on the hill of Tepeyac near Mexico City in December 1531. Speaking to
him in Nahuatl, his own language, she identified herself as the mother of the true God and asked that a
church be built on the site so that she might show her love and compassion to all who sought her.

Juan Diego carried her request to the archbishop, Juan de Zumarraga, who asked for a sign. When Juan
Diego's uncle, Juan Bernardino, fell gravely ill, Juan Diego tried to avoid the apparition out of
shame; but the Virgin met him, assured him that his uncle was already healed, and spoke the words now
inscribed over the basilica's entrance: "Am I not here, I who am your mother?" She told him to gather
flowers from the normally barren hilltop, where he found Castilian roses blooming out of season. When
Juan Diego opened his tilma before the archbishop to present them, the roses fell to the ground and the
now-famous image of the Virgin was found imprinted on the cloth.

## The image

The image on the tilma, a coarse cloak woven from agave fiber, has been the subject of enduring
devotion and study for nearly five centuries. To the indigenous peoples of Mexico it also spoke in a
visual language they could read: the woman stands upon the moon and is clothed with the sun, yet her
bowed head and folded hands show her as a humble servant rather than a goddess, pointing beyond herself
to the God she carries. Several features in particular are cited in the devotional tradition as marks
of its significance:

- **Its endurance.** A cloak of agave fiber would ordinarily decay within a few decades, yet the tilma
  has survived for nearly five hundred years, including long years hung unprotected and exposed to
  candle smoke, incense, and the touch of countless pilgrims before it was placed behind glass.
- **The absence of ordinary technique.** Examiners have reported that the central image shows no
  under-sketch and no visible brush strokes, and that the color sits directly on the unprepared,
  unsized cloth in a way that does not match the known painting methods of the period.
- **The eyes.** Under magnification, the Virgin's eyes are said to behave less like paint than like
  living eyes: researchers report that they curve and catch light as a real cornea would, and that each
  eye holds the same cluster of tiny human figures, positioned as a genuine reflection would be. Some
  interpret these figures as the group of people present in the room at the moment the tilma was
  unfurled, mirrored in the Virgin's gaze.
- **Its survival of accidents.** The tilma is said to have come through later hazards, including a
  corrosive nitric-acid spill in 1785 and a bomb set off near the image in 1921, with little or no
  damage to the cloth or the figure.

These claims are venerated in tradition and have inspired a long literature of investigation, but they
are not the basis of the Church's approval. The image is honored above all as the sign that drew a
people to Christ.

## How the Church regards it

Devotion spread quickly and was fostered by the Church in the centuries that followed. Pope Benedict
XIV named Our Lady of Guadalupe patroness of New Spain in 1754, the image received a canonical
coronation in 1895, and the shrine was raised to a minor basilica in 1976. Juan Diego was beatified in
1990 and canonized by Pope John Paul II in 2002. Our Lady of Guadalupe is patroness of the Americas,
and her feast is celebrated on December 12.

## Why it matters

Guadalupe is remembered above all for its fruit: in the decades after 1531, millions of the indigenous
peoples of Mexico entered the Church, and the Gospel met the peoples of the Americas in their own
image and tongue. As with every private revelation, the Church presents it as an aid to the faith
already handed down, not an addition to it.
