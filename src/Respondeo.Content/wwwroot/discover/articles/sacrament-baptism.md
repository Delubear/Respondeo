**What it is.**
Baptism is the doorway into the whole sacramental life.
Through water and the words "I baptize you in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit," 
a person is joined to Christ's death and resurrection, cleansed of sin, filled with grace, and made a member of His body.
It is not primarily something *we* do; it is something done *to* us: a rebirth we receive.

## The deep meaning: dying and rising with Christ

Baptism is far more than a washing. The water signifies two things at once: death and life. Going down
into it, the old self is buried with Christ; coming up from it, a new self rises with Him. St. Paul
makes the image explicit:

> Do you not know that all of us who have been baptized into Christ Jesus were baptized into his
> death? We were buried therefore with him by baptism into death, in order that, just as Christ was
> raised from the dead by the glory of the Father, we too might walk in newness of life.
>
> &mdash; Romans 6:3&ndash;4

This is why the Church calls Baptism a *rebirth*. Jesus told Nicodemus, "unless one is born of water
and the Spirit, he cannot enter the kingdom of God" (John 3:5). In it, original sin and every personal
sin committed beforehand are wholly forgiven, the Holy Spirit comes to dwell in the soul, and the
person becomes a true child of God and heir of heaven: not by metaphor, but in reality.

## How it is done

A sacrament has a visible sign made of *matter* and *form*. For Baptism the matter is natural water,
and the form is the Trinitarian words spoken while the water is applied, either by pouring it three
times over the head or by immersion. The essential words come from Christ's own command:

> Go therefore and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the
> Son and of the Holy Spirit.
>
> &mdash; Matthew 28:19

Aquinas explains that plain water is the proper and necessary matter
([*Summa Theologiae* III, Q. 66, A. 4](summa/tertia-q066#article-4)), and that this exact Trinitarian
form is required for validity ([*Summa Theologiae* III, Q. 66, A. 5](summa/tertia-q066#article-5)).
Both pouring and immersion are valid ways of applying it
([*Summa Theologiae* III, Q. 66, A. 7](summa/tertia-q066#article-7)). Get the matter and form right,
with the intention to do what the Church does, and the sacrament is truly conferred.

## Who can baptize

The ordinary minister is a bishop, priest, or deacon. But because Baptism is necessary for salvation,
the Church teaches that *in a case of necessity* **anyone** can baptize &mdash; even a non-believer &mdash; provided
they use water, say the Trinitarian words, and intend to do what the Church does (CCC 1256). This is
why a nurse, a parent, or a bystander may validly baptize a dying infant when no minister can be found.

## Why it cannot be done twice

Baptism, like Confirmation and Holy Orders, imprints an indelible spiritual mark &mdash; a *character* &mdash; on
the soul that configures the person to Christ forever. Because that seal can never be erased, it can
never be repeated:

> There is one body and one Spirit... one Lord, one faith, one baptism.
>
> &mdash; Ephesians 4:4&ndash;5

Aquinas grounds this in the nature of the character itself: it cannot be blotted out from the soul
([*Summa Theologiae* III, Q. 63, A. 5](summa/tertia-q063#article-5)), and so Baptism may not be
reiterated ([*Summa Theologiae* III, Q. 66, A. 9](summa/tertia-q066#article-9)). Even someone who falls
into grave sin and later returns is not re-baptized; they are restored through Confession, because the
baptismal seal was never lost. The Catechism states it plainly: "Baptism seals the Christian with the
indelible spiritual mark of his belonging to Christ... given once for all, Baptism cannot be repeated"
(CCC 1272).

## Conditional baptism

Precisely because Baptism cannot be repeated, the Church is careful never to re-baptize someone who has
already received it. Yet sometimes there is genuine, reasonable *doubt* about whether a valid Baptism
ever took place: for example, when records are lost, when it is unclear whether water truly flowed, or
when a convert's former community did not use the Trinitarian form. In those cases the Church may confer
a *conditional* Baptism, using the form "If you are not baptized, I baptize you in the name of the
Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit."

This is not a second Baptism. The condition means the sacrament takes effect only if the first one was
in fact invalid or never happened; if the person was already truly baptized, nothing new is conferred.
Conditional Baptism is reserved for real doubt, not scruples, and is never used simply to "make sure."

## Baptism of desire and of blood

Because God's grace is not confined to the sacraments He instituted, the Church recognizes that the
*effect* of Baptism can be received without the water itself in certain cases. Someone who desires
Baptism but dies before receiving it &mdash; explicitly, or implicitly through sincere love of God and the
will to do His will &mdash; can be saved by the **Baptism of desire**. A person who suffers death for the
faith before being baptized receives the **Baptism of blood** (CCC 1258&ndash;1260).

Aquinas already distinguished these three "baptisms" of Water, Blood, and the Spirit
([*Summa Theologiae* III, Q. 66, A. 11](summa/tertia-q066#article-11)), and taught that a person can be
saved without the sacrament of water when it is impossible to receive, through desire for it
([*Summa Theologiae* III, Q. 68, A. 2](summa/tertia-q068#article-2)). None of this makes water Baptism
optional for those who can receive it &mdash; the Church still urges it without delay &mdash; but it assures us that
no one who truly seeks God is lost for want of what they could not obtain.

## What it means for the one coming home

Everything begins here. In Baptism a person is made a new creation and a child of God, the stain of
original sin and all personal sin is washed away, and the very life of God is planted in the soul. This
is why the Church rejoices over a baptism the way a family rejoices over a birth: a new life has begun
that death cannot end. For the one entering the faith from outside, this is the threshold the whole path
has been leading toward, and, once crossed, it marks the soul forever.
