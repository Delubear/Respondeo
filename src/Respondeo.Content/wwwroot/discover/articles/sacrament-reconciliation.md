**What it is.**
In Reconciliation &mdash; also called Confession or Penance &mdash; the risen Christ exercises the power He gave His apostles: "Whose sins you forgive are forgiven them."
The penitent confesses their sins to a priest, expresses sorrow, and receives absolution: Christ's own forgiveness, spoken aloud, with certainty.

**Why celebrate it.**
This is the sacrament that reopens the door for anyone who has wandered.
It exists not to shame but to free.
Where a private "I'm sorry" leaves us wondering whether we are truly forgiven, Confession gives the answer out loud: *you are forgiven*, on the authority of Christ Himself.
It restores the grace lost through serious sin and heals the smaller wounds of daily failure.
This is why it is the natural first step home: not a hurdle to clear, but the mercy the sacrament was made to give.

There is also a human reason. Sin spoken aloud loses its power to hide. Naming a fault to another person &mdash; under the absolute seal of the confessional &mdash; is humbling in a way that private regret is not, and the words *I absolve you* answer the doubt that private prayer can leave unhealed: *am I really forgiven?*

## Where the sacrament comes from

To forgive or retain, the Apostles had to hear what was done: which presumes confession.

## What actually happens

The priest acts *in persona Christi* &mdash; in the person of Christ &mdash; as the instrument through whom Christ's own absolution is given.
