St. Bernadette Soubirous, the visionary of Lourdes, died in 1879 at the convent of Nevers. Her body is
among the well-known cases of an incorrupt body venerated in the Catholic tradition.

## What happened

Bernadette's body was exhumed three times as part of the process toward her canonization — in 1909,
1919, and 1925. On each occasion it was reported to be remarkably preserved. Today her body lies in a
glass reliquary at the Chapel of Saint-Gildard in Nevers, where it can be seen by pilgrims.

## What "incorrupt" means and does not mean

The Church attaches no doctrinal weight to bodily incorruption, and it is careful about the term. A
light wax coating was applied to the face and hands for display, and natural conditions can slow
decomposition. Incorruption is regarded as a notable phenomenon associated with some saints, not as
proof of sanctity — sanctity is judged by a life of heroic virtue, not by the state of a body.

## How the Church regards it

Bernadette was canonized in 1933 on the basis of her holiness and the examination of her cause, not
because of the condition of her remains. The incorruption is venerated by tradition as a sign, while
the canonization rests on other grounds.

## Why it matters

The case illustrates the Church's measured approach: it venerates a striking sign without overstating
it, and it grounds sainthood in a person's life rather than in a marvel about their body.
