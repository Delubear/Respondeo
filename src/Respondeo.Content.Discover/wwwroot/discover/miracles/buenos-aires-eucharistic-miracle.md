The Buenos Aires case is a modern reported Eucharistic miracle, often discussed alongside Lanciano
because of the claimed similarity of the tissue analysed.

## What happened

In 1996, a discarded consecrated host was placed in water to dissolve, as is the custom. Days later it
was reported to have turned into a bloody substance. The then-Archbishop of Buenos Aires, Jorge Mario
Bergoglio (later Pope Francis), had the sample photographed and preserved, and years later submitted
it for analysis.

## The scientific findings

Laboratory analysis reportedly identified the sample as human cardiac muscle tissue showing signs of
inflammation, with white blood cells present. Commentators have noted the reported resemblance to the
Lanciano relic. As with all such analyses, the results are contested and have not been independently
replicated under controlled conditions.

## How the Church regards it

The case has not received a definitive formal declaration and is best described as reported and
studied rather than officially approved. The Church counsels prudence with alleged miracles and does
not oblige belief in them.

## Why it matters

Like Lanciano, the account is presented as a sign pointing to the doctrine of the Real Presence,
offered for the strengthening of faith rather than as its foundation.
