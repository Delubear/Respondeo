Lourdes, in southern France, is the site of the 1858 apparitions of the Virgin Mary to St. Bernadette
Soubirous, and has since become one of the most visited pilgrimage shrines in the world, known
especially for reported healings.

## What happened

Following the apparitions, a spring emerged at the grotto, and pilgrims began reporting cures. Over
the decades, thousands of claimed healings have been submitted to the shrine.

## How the cures are examined

Uniquely, Lourdes maintains a rigorous, deliberately sceptical process. A claimed cure is first
studied by the Lourdes Medical Bureau, drawing on physicians of any faith or none. Cases that appear
medically inexplicable are referred to the International Medical Committee of Lourdes. Only after this
scrutiny may a cure be declared medically unexplained; a separate judgment by the person's own bishop
is required before it is recognized as miraculous.

## The results

Millions of pilgrims have come, an enormous number of cures have been claimed, and after this severe
filtering the Church has formally recognized around 70 as miraculous. The small proportion is itself
part of the point: the process is designed to weed out the ordinary, the psychosomatic, and the
premature.

## Why it matters

Lourdes offers a striking example of the Church subjecting reported miracles to independent medical
scrutiny rather than assuming them. The recognized cures are presented as signs of God's mercy, while
the shrine's deeper work is the consolation of the sick who come whether or not they are cured.
