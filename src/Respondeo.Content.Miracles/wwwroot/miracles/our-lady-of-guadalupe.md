Our Lady of Guadalupe is one of the most widely venerated Marian apparitions, formally recognized by
the Church and profoundly significant to the evangelization of the Americas.

## What happened

According to the tradition recorded in the Nican Mopohua, in December 1531 the Virgin Mary appeared
several times to St. Juan Diego, an indigenous convert, on the hill of Tepeyac near Mexico City. She
asked that a church be built on the site. As a sign for the doubting bishop, she caused Castilian
roses to bloom out of season; when Juan Diego opened his tilma to present them, the now-famous image
of the Virgin was found imprinted on the cloth.

## The image

The image on the tilma has been the subject of much devotion and study. Believers point to the
survival of the coarse agave-fiber cloth over centuries and to details of the image as marks of its
significance. Such claims are venerated in tradition and are not the basis of the Church's approval.

## How the Church regards it

The apparitions are formally recognized, and Juan Diego was canonized in 2002. Our Lady of Guadalupe
is patroness of the Americas, and her feast is celebrated on December 12.

## Why it matters

Guadalupe is remembered above all for its fruit: the conversion of millions and the meeting of the
Gospel with the peoples of the Americas. As with every private revelation, the Church presents it as
an aid to the faith already handed down, not an addition to it.
