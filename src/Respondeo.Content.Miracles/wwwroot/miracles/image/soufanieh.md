Our Lady of Soufanieh refers to a series of reported phenomena in the Soufanieh district of Damascus,
Syria, centered on Myrna Nazzour (also spelled Mirna), a young Greek Melkite Catholic woman, beginning
in November 1982. The events are notable for a message calling Christians to unity, especially around
a common celebration of Easter.

:::

## What happened

In November 1982 a small paper icon of Our Lady of Kazan in the Nazzour home was reported to exude
olive oil. Soon Myrna herself was said to exude oil from her hands and face. Over the following years
she reported apparitions of the Virgin Mary and of Christ, and on several occasions bore stigmata,
frequently coinciding with Holy Week.

## The message

The central theme attributed to the apparitions is Christian unity: a repeated plea that the divided
Christian communities reconcile and celebrate Easter on a single date. The messages emphasize love,
prayer, and the unity of the Church, addressed to Catholic and Orthodox faithful alike.

## How the Church regards it

Soufanieh has drawn wide attention and pilgrimage in the Middle East, and local clergy have observed
the events closely. It has not received formal approval as an authenticated apparition, and it is
treated here as a reported, still-unadjudicated case. As with every private revelation, the Church
neither requires belief nor presents such events as an addition to the deposit of faith.

## Why it matters

The Soufanieh phenomena are remembered above all for their ecumenical message in a region marked by
ancient Christian divisions. Whatever the final judgment, the call to unity around the Risen Christ
echoes the Church's own longing that all may be one.
