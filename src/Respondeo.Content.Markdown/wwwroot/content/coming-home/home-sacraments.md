Everything the Church offers converges here.
When we speak of grace, communion, and the life of God given to human beings, we are finally speaking of the **sacraments** — the concrete acts by which Christ touches a person through the Church.

A sacrament is, in the classic definition, *an outward sign of an inward grace, instituted by Christ.*
That is, it is a visible action — washing, anointing, eating, forgiving, joining — that does not merely *represent* grace but actually *conveys* it.
God, who made us body and soul, deals with us bodily.
He does not leave His grace to float as an idea; He attaches it to water and oil and bread so that we can receive it and *know* we have.

**Why seven?**
The Church recognizes seven sacraments because they answer to the whole shape of a human life and its supernatural counterpart:

- Some **initiate** us into the life of grace — Baptism, Confirmation, and the Eucharist.
- Some **heal** what sin and suffering damage — Reconciliation and the Anointing of the Sick.
- Some are **at the service of communion**, ordering a person's life toward others — Holy Orders and Matrimony.

Together they accompany a Christian from birth to death: born in the font, strengthened in Confirmation, fed in the Eucharist, restored in Confession, healed in Anointing, and — through Orders or Marriage — given a vocation that pours life back out to others.

## Why celebrate each one

The sacraments are not obligations to be endured but gifts to be *celebrated*, because in each one it is Christ Himself who acts.
