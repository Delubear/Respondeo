> On the unity of God, see [*Summa Theologiae* I, Q. 11, A. 3](summa/prima-q011#article-3): "Whether God is one?"

The arguments for God's existence do not merely reach *a* first cause; they reach a first cause of a very particular kind — one whose existence is not received, not limited, not measured out. Once we see *that* kind of being, the question "why not several of them?" answers itself.

The ancient world assumed many gods; much of the modern world assumes that "how many gods" is a matter of taste or tradition. Reason says otherwise. The very features that made this being the ultimate explanation are the features that make it necessarily **one**.

**The argument, step by step:**

1. What the earlier ways reach is a being whose existence is not limited or received — Being Itself, not a being that merely *has* being alongside others.
2. To have *two* such beings, there would need to be some difference between them — something one had that the other lacked.
3. But a difference is a limit: to lack anything is to be measured, bounded, not unlimited.
4. An unlimited being lacks nothing, so there is no room for a second one to differ from the first.
5. Therefore the ultimate source cannot be many. It is necessarily **one** &mdash; *and this all men speak of as God.*

**Ruling out the rivals.** This closes two doors at once. **Polytheism** — many finite gods dividing up the cosmos — fails, because finite, limited beings are exactly the kind of thing that still needs explaining; they are candles, not the flame's source. **Dualism** — two ultimate principles, one good and one evil, locked in eternal contest — fails too, because two ultimates would each have to be limited by the other, and a limited ultimate is a contradiction.

## See it today: the idea of "the biggest number"

Ask a child to name the biggest possible number and they will try — a million, a billion, a googol — and each time you can add one. There is no *two* biggest numbers competing for the title, and not because we have not looked hard enough. It is that "biggest" leaves no room for a rival: whatever fell short of it would not be the biggest, and whatever equalled it would just be it.

Unlimited being works the same way, only it is real rather than a mental limit. You cannot have two infinities of existence side by side, each missing what the other has. The moment you say "unlimited," you have said "one."

**Where this leads.** So reason does not merely permit monotheism; it *requires* it. The ultimate source of all being is single, undivided, and unrepeatable. But knowing the source is one still leaves the deepest question untouched: is this one God a *someone* or a *something* — a mind that knows and a will that loves, or a blind, impersonal principle? That is the next step.
