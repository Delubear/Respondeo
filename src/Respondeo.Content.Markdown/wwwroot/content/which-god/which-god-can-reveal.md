> "It was necessary for man's salvation that there should be a knowledge revealed by God, besides philosophical science built up by human reason." &mdash; [*Summa Theologiae* I, Q. 1, A. 1](summa/prima-q001#article-1)

Reason has carried us to a God who is one, and who is personal — a mind and a will. That last point changes everything. A blind force cannot address you. A person can. So the door stands open to something reason alone could never supply: that this God might **speak**.

Two questions follow. First, *could* He? Second, if He did, how would we ever **recognize** it as His voice rather than wishful thinking or human invention?

**That revelation is possible.** If God is the personal author of minds, then reaching those minds is not beyond Him — the maker of speech is not mute. There is nothing incoherent in the idea of the Creator communicating with creatures who can know and be known. Reason does not prove that God *has* spoken, but it shows there is no barrier in principle: revelation is *possible*, and given a God who knows and loves, even *fitting*.

**Why it might be needed.** Reason can reach God, but slowly, partially, and mixed with error — the arguments here take real work, and few have the leisure to complete them. If the truths about God matter for every person's life, not just for philosophers, then it is fitting that God make them available more surely and more widely than unaided reason ever could. Revelation completes reason; it does not compete with it.

**How we would recognize it: the motives of credibility.** A genuine word from God would not arrive as a bare claim — "trust me." It would come with **signs** that no merely human source could counterfeit: 

- **Fulfilled prophecy** — knowledge of events long before they occur, beyond any human forecast.
- **Miracles** — actions that suspend the ordinary course of nature, which only the Author of nature can perform.
- **The transforming holiness** it produces, and its inner coherence across centuries and cultures.

These do not *force* belief, but they make it **reasonable** — they are the credentials by which reason can identify a claim as truly from God rather than from men.

## See it today: recognizing a real signature

Anyone can *say* they speak for the king. What separates the true envoy from the impostor is credentials the impostor cannot fake — the royal seal, the king's own hand, actions only the crown could authorize. You do not believe the messenger because he is loud; you believe him because he carries what only the sender could give.

Revelation works the same way. The question is never merely "does this text make grand claims?" — anyone can. It is "does it carry the seal — the prophecy, the wonders, the fruit — that only God could stamp?"

**Where this leads.** So a personal God *can* reveal Himself, and there are marks by which a real revelation could be known. But many voices in history claim to carry that seal — Islam, deism, the great Eastern traditions, and the faith of Israel among them. The next step is to weigh the claimants: how does the God reason has reached relate to these, and where do the credentials actually hold?
